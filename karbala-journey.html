Chapter One · Present Day

The Living Shrine

Karbala, Iraq 2026 CE

Every year, tens of millions walk toward Karbala for Arbaeen one of the largest peaceful gatherings on Earth. They come on foot, from every direction, past free roadside kitchens run by strangers for strangers.

At the center stand two golden domes: the shrine of Imam Hussain and, close by, the shrine of his brother Abbas. Scroll down to see how this pilgrimage began.

Chapter Two · The Land

A Plain by the Euphrates

Karbala, on the western bank of the Euphrates

Long before the domes, Karbala was open desert plain a stop along caravan routes near the river. Its name is said to combine "karb," hardship, and "bala," trial: a plain that would come to hold both.

Chapter Three · Ahlulbait

The Household of the Prophet

Medina, decades before Karbala

Ahlulbait the Prophet Muhammad's household is remembered for a fazilat, a nobility of character, spoken of across Islamic tradition: Ali's justice, Fatima's patience, and the closeness of their sons Hassan and Hussain to their grandfather.

Hussain ibn Ali was born into this house. Move forward in the story to see the choice that would define him.

Chapter Four · The Journey

The Road to Karbala

1 Muharram, 61 AH c. 680 CE

Asked to pledge allegiance to a ruler he believed had abandoned his grandfather's principles, Hussain refused. He set out from Medina toward Kufa with his family and a small company around seventy companions and kin and made camp on the plain of Karbala.

There, the caravan was surrounded and cut off from the river. Ten days later, on Ashura, it would end.

Chapter Five · Ashura

The Tenth Day

10 Muharram, 61 AH c. 680 CE

Vastly outnumbered and denied water for three days, Hussain's companions rode out one by one rather than surrender. His horse, Zuljanah, is remembered as returning to camp riderless the moment his family understood what had happened.

Chapter Six · The Legacy

Zainab Carries the Word

From 61 AH to the present

Hussain's sister Zainab survived Karbala and spoke of what she had witnessed in the courts of Kufa and Damascus, turning a massacre in the desert into a story that could not be silenced.

That telling is why, this year, millions will walk to Karbala again. The journey you began at the top is the same one this page just told in reverse.
