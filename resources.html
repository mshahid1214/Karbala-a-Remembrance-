Further reading

A short shelf

This site is a doorway, not a destination. Classical Shia historiography on Karbala forms its own genre the maqtal, literally "the place of killing" and the books and libraries below are a reasonable place to keep going.

Book · Maqtal literature

Nafasul Mahmoom

Shaykh Abbas Qummi

A widely cited Shia account of Karbala's tragedy, compiled from earlier historical sources one of the standard references still read from in majlis gatherings today.

Read at al-islam.org → Book · Essay collection

Imam Husayn, the Saviour of Islam

Compiled by Sayyid Muhammad Rizvi

A collection of essays by various scholars on the significance of Ashura and the martyrdom of Hussain and his companions.

Read at al-islam.org → Book · Lectures

Martyrdom: Arise and Bear Witness

Ali Shariati

Lectures by the Iranian sociologist and public intellectual on the concept and role of martyrdom in Shia thought.

Read at al-islam.org → Book · Dialogue

Peshawar Nights

Sultan al-Wa'izin Shirazi

A recorded ten-night dialogue between Sunni and Shia scholars in 1927, covering succession, the Ahl al-Bayt, and related questions useful background for readers new to the Sunni–Shia divide.

Read at al-islam.org → Digital library

Al-Islam.org

Ahlul Bayt Digital Islamic Library Project

A large, free, volunteer-run library of Shia texts in translation the source for most of the books linked on this page, and a good place to browse further.

Visit al-islam.org → Encyclopedia

WikiShia

Encyclopedia of the World Ahl al-Bayt Assembly

An English-language reference encyclopedia on Shia Islam figures, events, terminology, and books, including detailed entries on Karbala's participants and sources.

Visit wikishia.net → Reference article

Battle of Karbala

Encyclopaedia Britannica

A concise, independent academic overview of the battle's causes, events, and historical aftermath.

Read at britannica.com → Reference article

Battle of Karbala

World History Encyclopedia

Another independent historical overview, useful for cross-checking dates and the sequence of events against the account on this site's history page.

Read at worldhistory.org →

None of these sources will fully agree on every detail — that's normal for events this old and this contested. Reading two or three side by side is more useful than treating any single one, including this site, as final.
